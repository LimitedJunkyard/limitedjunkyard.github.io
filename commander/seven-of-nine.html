Seven of Nine: the dilemma loop

A blue-black combo deck. Three cheap cards make Seven of Nine draw your whole library, and one more card turns the empty library into a win. Everything else in the deck finds those cards.

The loop is ready by turn seven a little more often (74% instead of 71%).

How it wins

  1. Tap it: a land goes from your hand onto the battlefield.

  2. The land enters and returns itself to your hand.

  3. A land entered, so you choose a mode: untap the Atlas. That choice is a dilemma.

  4. You faced a dilemma, so you draw a card. Tap the Atlas again.

  5. Basics you draw go onto the battlefield the same way and stay. Tap three for the Maniac when you draw him, keep looping, and when the library is empty the next draw wins.

Putting a land onto the battlefield with an ability isn't playing a land, so the one-land-a-turn rule doesn't apply. Every pass draws a card, and that draw isn't optional: without the Maniac in play, stop while the library still has cards. Elim Garak is the other way to win, and Rescue is the way out if it goes wrong.

15,000 simulated games per list, one player and no opponents, mana colors ignored: a ceiling for comparing the lists, not a win rate. Counterspells do nothing in a game with no opponents, so the lists with more of them look a little worse late. "Ready" means all three loop pieces and the mana to start that turn. It's an infinite combo: tell your table before the game.

Commander

1 card

Every time you choose a mode, on a spell or on an ability, she draws you a card. The whole deck is built to make that choice free and endless.

Loop 1: land dropper

2 cards

A creature that taps to put a land from your hand onto the battlefield. It has to be in play a turn before it can tap.

Loop 2: untapper

2 cards

When a land enters, these make you choose a mode, and one mode untaps the dropper. That choice is the dilemma. Only these two exist in blue and black.

Loop 3: the land comes back

2 cards

The dropper needs the same land in hand again. Three lands below return themselves; these two return any land, paid for with the mana the lands just made.

The kill

2 cards

The loop puts as much of the library in your hand as you want; one of these two turns that into a win. Without Laboratory Maniac in play, leave a few cards in the library: Seven's draw is not optional, and a dilemma with an empty library loses the game.

Finder: a two-mana piece

6 cards

Each of these becomes Walking Atlas, PuPu UFO, Trade Routes or Oboro Breezecaller. Transmute: pay three and discard the card to search for a card with the same mana value.

Finder: a three-mana piece

4 cards

Each of these becomes an untapper, Garak or Laboratory Maniac. The untapper is the role with the fewest copies, so these matter most.

Finder: the land

2 cards

A self-returning land has to be in hand on the winning turn. Tolaria West, in the lands, does the same job.

Finder: any card

11 cards

Whatever is missing. The cheap ones go first; the five-mana ones are for the long game.

Card selection

11 cards

Cheap looks at the top of the library, to hit land drops early and dig for pieces later.

Protection

8 cards

Answers for the turn you win. Foil costs no mana, so nothing has to be held back for it. The modal ones also draw a card with Seven of Nine in play. Rescue is the one that must not be modal: it is the way out when the library is empty.

Mana

11 cards

The loop costs about ten mana across three turns, plus finders. Ten rocks get Seven of Nine out a turn early.

Lands

38 cards

38 lands. The first three are loop pieces: they return themselves to hand when they enter. While the loop runs, every basic you draw goes into play untapped through the dropper, and that mana casts the kill. Stop before the library is empty: every pass draws a card.

Plus 12 Island and 11 Swamp.

One more idea: a kill that removal can't stop

loop ready by turn 7: 70%

With fifty cards in hand, Skirge Familiar turns every discarded card into a black mana, and that pays for Exsanguinate. It is a mana ability, so nobody can respond to the discards; only a counterspell stops it. It needs no dilemma, so it also works with an empty library. Two slots, which is why it is not in the list.
