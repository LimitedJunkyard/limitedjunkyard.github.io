Q, Stacking the Deck: four ways to break him

When Q enters, you shuffle your library and exile cards from the top until you hit one that isn't a land. That card gains an ability: exile a card from your hand, make a copy of it, and cast the copy, as many times as you can pay. The catch is the shuffle. These four decks take the luck out: Q finds Glaring Fleshraker every single time, and five copies of it kill the whole table.

It's a combo deck: tell your table before the game. Prices: the cheapest printing of each card, Scryfall, 22 November 2026; basic lands not counted.

How five copies kill everyone

  1. Every copy is a colorless spell, so each Fleshraker already on the battlefield makes an Eldrazi Spawn.
  2. Every colorless creature that enters makes each Fleshraker deal 1 damage to each opponent. That counts the Spawns and the new Fleshraker.
  3. So copies 1 to 5 deal 0, 2, 6, 12 and 20 damage: 40 to each opponent.
  4. From the fourth copy on, the Spawns pay for the next one, so the only limit is cards in hand.

The copies are cast (rule 707.12), so Drannith Magistrate stops all four decks: your opponents can't cast spells from anywhere but their hand, and Q becomes a four-mana creature. Kill her first, or don't start.

The video's $35.42 counts the 93 basic lands too (12 Wastes at about $0.69, 81 Mountains at about $0.08).

Q skips lands, so this deck gives him 97 of them and exactly two spells: Glaring Fleshraker, the card that wins, and Twinflame, the card that re-rolls.

If Q hits Twinflame, copy it onto Q. A copy of Q enters and shuffles again, and now the only spell left in the library is Fleshraker.

Every copy costs a card from your hand, and here that's a Mountain. For once, a hand full of Mountains is exactly what you want.

Q comes down on turn four. One rule: if Fleshraker is in your opening hand, mulligan it away. Q can only find it in the library.

Q is the commander, and this time you choose the card during the game.

Leveler: when it enters, your whole library is exiled. Normally, that's just how you lose.

Then Mirror of Fate: sacrifice it, choose up to seven cards from exile, and they become your library. Fleshraker, and six lands. Then Q comes in, and there's exactly one spell for him to find.

The problem is that it needs two specific cards, so it comes together in about one game in five, by turn fourteen.

Rakdos, with L'Rell from the same Star Trek set as the commander, and Q in the 99.

Doomsday: search your library for five cards, exile the rest, and stack those five on top. Your five are Fleshraker and four lands. One card does the whole setup, and black has thirteen budget tutors to find it.

But now your library is five cards. Q exiles the lands above Fleshraker, the copies need nine mana and five cards in hand, and nothing new comes in. Most of the lost games are lost by drawing from an empty library.

Don't you lose if you draw your whole deck? No: you lose when you draw from an empty library, and the loop stops with three cards left. When the mana for the finish is short, it stops with eight left and finishes next turn.

Yes, with your whole deck in hand you could win a dozen other ways. You put Fleshraker back and let Q pick it anyway.

It has thirteen ways to find Top, ten cards that make it cost one less, and seventeen mana rocks.

The combo, step by step

  1. You may cast noncreature spells from the top of your library.

  2. Tap it: draw a card, and Top goes on top of your library.

  3. Elsha casts Top again, and with anything that makes it cost one less, it's free. Again and again: the whole deck comes into your hand.

  4. A red mana for every spell you cast, so the loop pays for everything after it.

  5. Stop with three cards left. Brainstorm draws them and puts two back: Fleshraker and a land.

  6. Q shuffles a library of two, skips the land, and finds Fleshraker. Every card in your hand is a copy.

Cheaper: $171.58, table dead by turn 7 in 78% of games
